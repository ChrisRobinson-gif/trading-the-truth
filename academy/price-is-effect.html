Part 1 · Lesson 3

Price is the effect; participation is the cause

Candles are a summary of what already happened. They are not the cause. Participation — who showed size, who aggressed, who absorbed — is the cause.

Hard line

Retail culture worships the candle. Engulfing. Doji. “Bullish close.” Fine as shorthand. Useless as edge when you treat ink as prophecy.

Price moved because orders matched. Somebody lifted the offer or hit the bid. Somebody rested and got run over — or absorbed and held. That fight is participation. The candle is the press release after the meeting.

Data is king. Oscillator soup is not. Candles summarise; they don’t explain.

What to look at instead of folklore

  • Where did size trade — not just that a green bar printed.
  • Who was aggressive — marketable buys vs sells (Bid×Ask language).
  • Did aggression move price, or get absorbed?
  • Context — session, overnight inventory, accepted value vs imbalance.

You do not need a tip wire for this. You need literacy. ATAS is the classroom stack for reading that fight. CQG is Chris’s personal aside — not the product pitch.

Risk of the candle cult

If your edge is “pattern name + hope,” you will buy confluence packs, join rooms that clap for screenshots, and confuse sim green with skill. That is retail theatre. We kill it in Part 2.

Execute (today)

  1. Pick one RTH session. For three swings, write: candle story vs participation story.
  2. Snap search: “aggression”, “clusters”, “candles”. Read dry. No hype.
  3. If you only have candles today — stand aside on live size. Literacy first.
